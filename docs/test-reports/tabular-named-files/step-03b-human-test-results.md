### What this shows

1. **The Sources lines were truthful for every model**: `structure seulement` where the window was too small for a sample (4 096 tokens: the sample share, scaled down with the window, holds no row), `1 ligne sur 35854` where one row was all the share allowed.
2. **The remark of the owner is founded in its observation, not in its explanation.**
   - *Small models are weaker on spreadsheets; under 1b they cannot:* confirmed on two samples (`qwen3:0.6b`, `granite3.1-moe:1b`), both unusable. But two models of 1.5 to 1.7 billion parameters (`qwen2.5:1.5b`, `qwen3:1.7b`) describe the structure correctly, and **none of the three small models of 4 096 tokens received a single row**: what was tested for them is structure only, not data. Size alone does not explain it: `granite3.1-moe:3b` (3 billion parameters, but a mixture of experts with few active parameters) is the worst of the nine.
   - *"They are optimised for text":* not testable here and not proved by these answers; the observed causes are the number of active parameters and the poor following of a long structured prompt. It stays a hypothesis.
   - *The window matters for the time, especially with tabular data:* **partly founded, not measured.** The times above rise with the window for the three models measured (13 s at 8 192, 16 s and 22 s at 12 288, 47 s at 16 384), and there is a real mechanism: the cards, the sample and the profile are sized in proportion to the window (`scaled_over_baseline`), so a larger window sends a larger turn, and table text (numbers, separators) holds more tokens per character than prose, so the same number of characters costs more to read. But the figures are confounded: the tower holds two models at a time and a change of model includes a load (the `gemma4:e4b` first answers took 80 to 90 s in the measure), `granite3.1-moe:3b`'s 43 s is a runaway generation and not a long prompt, and the 4 096-token models are slow (20 to 31 s) with the shortest turn. To confirm: time the prompt processing apart from the generation for one question on a warm model at 4 different window sizes. Not done.
3. **Three defects to carry forward** (no code written):
   - *A sample of one row is not a sample.* With four tables on the default window the big file gets `1 ligne sur 35854` (`qwen3:4b-instruct`, `granite3.1-moe:1b`): the line is honest but the row tells the model nothing. The sharing should give a sheet either a useful minimum of rows or none (and the profile still carries the figures). Step 10 (it extends this sample) or a small fix of 03b.
   - *No guard against a repetition loop* (`granite3.1-moe:3b`): the generation runs to the length limit and the user waits 43 s for a paragraph repeated twenty times. A repetition check on the stream, or a refusal to offer such models for this task, belongs to step 16 (model capability profile).
   - *A wrong claim made of true numbers is not caught* (`ministral-3:3b`, "même que les factures"): known limit (DECISIONS, N-5).
4. **For step 16 and step 25** (capability profile, model benchmark): on this tower, for the mixed tier with a sample, `gemma4:e2b`, `gemma4:e4b`, `ministral-3:3b` and `qwen3:4b-instruct` are usable; `qwen3:1.7b` and `qwen2.5:1.5b` give a correct description of the structure but, at 4 096 tokens, receive no row; `granite3.1-moe:1b`, `granite3.1-moe:3b` and `qwen3:0.6b` should not be offered for table questions. A window of 4 096 tokens is below the default window the shares are sized for (DECISIONS, step 03, row 11): the fixed shares of the instruction, the excerpts and the cards (about 10 000 characters) exceed its prompt (about 8 000), so the gateway may truncate the turn; step 16 decides what to do with such models.
